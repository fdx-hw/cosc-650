Week 8 research note: an integrated Elden Ring assistant

Inputs
Task: build one system that integrates at least three techniques from earlier weeks, evaluate it with concrete metrics and an LLM-as-judge pass on 10 to 20 held-out cases, and find and trace one failure. I built an Elden Ring build-and-lore assistant: it answers questions about weapons, bosses, talismans and spells from game data, uses tools for "can I wield this?" and weight arithmetic, and declines when the data does not cover a question.
Techniques reused: Week 5 retrieval (MiniLM embeddings + FAISS, one chunk per game record); Week 4 tool use (the guarded code-runner, validator and dispatcher copied in unchanged, plus two new tools, get_weapon_stats and check_requirements); Week 7 retrieval-selected dynamic few-shot, used as a router that sends each question down a lookup path or a tool path; Week 3 grounded prompt (answer only from the context and tool results, otherwise decline). The Week 6 dense + lexical re-ranker was used as the mitigation test.
Models: Claude Haiku (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) for generation, routing and the tool loop, in place of the starter's Gemini, the same swap disclosed in earlier weeks. The judge is a different Claude model (claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929), so the system is not grading its own writing; it is still the same model family. Temperature pinned to 0.
Data: 738 records from the Elden Ring Fan API (307 weapons, 106 bosses, 87 talismans, 98 incantations, 71 sorceries, 69 shields), community data scraped from fan sites. Fetched at run time and not committed.
Evaluation set: 60 routing questions and 18 end-to-end cases, all built from templates over real item names, with entities split by a hash of the name so router-train, router-eval and end-to-end questions never share an item. The router-eval and end-to-end questions use phrasings the router never saw. Gold answers were computed from the records before any model call. Three of the 18 cases ask for things the data cannot answer.
Mode: real API calls throughout. The notebook was first run against fakes (fake embedder, fake Claude, fake Fan API) to check the plumbing, which proves nothing about model behavior; every number below comes from the Colab run.

What I found
Routing: the few-shot router and a zero-shot prompt both got all 60 held-out questions right (no disagreements, p = 1.0), so this run does not show the Week 7 technique improving routing. A nearest-neighbor vote with no model scored 85%, and the starter's keyword router scored 50%, which is what always answering "lookup" would score on a balanced set. Keywords I wrote after seeing the eval phrasings reached 100%, so they prove only that the task is easy for a person who has seen the questions. I kept the few-shot router because integrating it was the point of the capstone, not because the data favored it.
End-to-end: 15 of 18 cases right by plain-code checks, 83.3% with a 95% interval of 60.8% to 94.2%. Requirements, weight lookup, boss location and the three unanswerable questions were all correct; boss drops were 1 of 2, "can I wield it" 2 of 3, and weight sums or differences 2 of 3. The judge scored correctness 4.33, grounding 4.89 and calibration 4.33 out of 5, and agreed with the plain-code check on every case (15 pass, 3 fail), though that is only 18 cases and both work from the same gold answers.
The failure: two of the three misses have the same shape. When retrieval did not return the record a question was about, the model did not call a tool, even though the tools look weapons up by name and would have answered. "Could someone with 21 Str and 13 Dex swing the Arbalest?" retrieved five unrelated clubs and polearms, logged no tool call, and answered that the Arbalest was not in the provided context. "By how much does the Cranial Vessel Candlestand outweigh the Dagger?" retrieved the Candlestand but three other daggers instead of the weapon named Dagger, made no tool call, and asked which dagger was meant. The ablation isolates the trigger: handing the assistant the correct records raised the score from 15 to 17 of 18, while forcing the correct route changed nothing (routing was already perfect).
So the weak point is the seam between components, not any one of them. Retrieval decided what the model saw, and the model treated "not in the context" as "not in the data" even on a path where it had a tool for exactly that. The same behavior is a calibration problem in the other direction from the one I tested: all three unanswerable questions were correctly declined, but a question the data could answer was declined just as readily when retrieval missed. Why the model skipped the tool is not shown by the first run. My guess was that the "answer only from the context" rule in the grounded prompt outweighs the instruction to use tools; the follow-up below tests it.
The third miss (boss drops for "Godskin Apostle And Godskin Noble") first looked unexplained, and a follow-up diagnostic that printed the answers resolved it. Under the full system, retrieval returned the individual bosses and Godskin Duo instead of the record with that exact name, and the answer merged their drops (the judge gave it correctness 1), which is a real retrieval error. Under oracle retrieval the answer was right in substance (70,000 Runes, 1 Godskin Swaddling Cloth, 1 Black Flame Ritual) but failed my plain-code check, which wants the data's wording "Godskin Swaddling Cloth x1". So "giving it the correct record did not fix it" was my check being strict, not the model being wrong, and the re-ranked run passed only because it happened to write "x1".
Mitigation tested: the Week 6 re-ranker raised the share of 76 questions with every needed record in the top 5 from 86.8% to 94.7% (6 gained, none lost, p = 0.031) and fixed two of the three failed cases (the Dagger question and the Godskin one), but not the Arbalest one. The questions contain full item names, which favors word overlap, so I would expect a smaller gain on how players actually type.

Follow-up: two fixes, tested
I wrote two fixes after seeing the failures, so the 18 original cases are not a fair test of them. For a fair test I built 48 new tool-path questions (can I wield it, weight sums and differences) from items none of the 18 cases use. Dense retrieval missed a named record on 12 of them, and those 12 are the test set; the selection used retrieval only, before any model call.
Baseline got 0 of 12. The Week 6 re-ranker got 5 of 12 (sign test p = 0.062). Exact-name linking, which adds any record whose full name appears in the question (longest match first, so "Dagger" does not also pull in "Bloodstained Dagger"), got 12 of 12 with none broken (p < 0.001). A tool-path rule that tells the model to look a named weapon up with the tool before declining got 8 of 12 (p = 0.008). On the original 18 cases the baseline was 15, the re-ranker 17, linking 18, the rule 16, and linking plus the rule 18, none of them breaking a passing case.
The rule is the more informative result about cause. With retrieval unchanged and only the system prompt different, the model called a tool when its record was missing on 9 of 12 questions, against 1 of 12 before. That fits my earlier guess that the "answer only from the context" rule was suppressing the tool, though it is one wording on 12 questions. The rule is not a full fix: three questions were still declined without a tool call (the missing item was a seal in two of them), and one called the calculator on a different Halberd from the one named. The Falchion was looked up correctly in one question and declined in another, so it is not simply missing data.
Linking is the fix I would ship first, because it removes the need for the tool call rather than asking the model to remember it. Its limits: the questions contain the full item name, which is the best case for exact matching, so misspellings and nicknames would still miss; "12 of 12" on a set selected for retrieval misses is nearly true by construction; and linking added a record the question did not need on 1 of 76 earlier probes (mean 1.25 linked records per question). I did not measure latency or cost.

Other takeaways
Test the guess, not just the fix: a prompt rule that changed tool use from 1 of 12 to 9 of 12 is stronger evidence about the cause than the first run's reasoning was.
Check any automatic stage diagnosis against the ablation and the printed answers before writing it up. My diagnose() blamed retrieval for all three failures; the ablation showed that was right for two and incomplete for the third, and printing the answers showed the third was partly my check rejecting a correct paraphrase.
Treat an LLM judge's agreement with plain-code checks on 18 cases as encouraging, not as validation, and keep the judge a different model from the generator. The judge model I used is deprecated and reaches end-of-life on November 30, 2026, so a re-run needs a new JUDGE_MODEL.
Remember what the gold answers measure here: they come from the same scraped records the assistant reads, so a high score means faithful to that data, not accurate about the game. Before relying on it, spot-check the records against the game, since the scraped data has gaps (for example, some scaling values appear as "?" and boss health as "???").
